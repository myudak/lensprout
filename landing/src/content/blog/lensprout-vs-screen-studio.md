Screen Studio helped popularize the auto-zoom screen recording: you record, and the video glides toward whatever you click, with a smooth cursor and a nice background. Lensprout brings a similar idea to a Chrome extension. They are built for different setups, so the right choice depends mostly on your computer and what you record.

Facts about Screen Studio below come from its website as it was on 18 October 2026. Check [screen.studio](https://screen.studio/) for current details.

## The short version

- **Pick Screen Studio** if you are on a Mac, record native apps as well as websites, and want polish such as cursor smoothing, webcam background removal and shareable links.
- **Pick Lensprout** if you are on Windows or Linux, mostly record web apps, or want a free and open source tool.

## Side by side

| Feature | Lensprout | Screen Studio |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Platform | Chrome extension (desktop Chrome on any OS) | macOS only, 13.1 or later recommended |
| What it records | A screen or window, chosen in Chrome's picker | Your Mac's screen, windows and apps |
| Automatic zoom | From clicks and typing on web pages | From what you did while recording, such as clicks |
| Cursor | Overlay cursor, highlight and click animations on top of the real one | Cursor smoothing and click effects |
| Webcam | Picture in picture, side by side, stacked, full or hidden | Several layouts, with on-device background removal |
| Microphone | Mixed into the recording, with Chrome's noise suppression | Standard, noise reduction and podcast modes |
| Export | MP4 up to 4K (where supported) and GIF up to 960 px wide | MP4 at 720p, 1080p or 4K up to 60 fps; GIFs up to 1080p |
| Shareable links | No | Yes |
| Price | Free, MIT open source | $9 a month billed yearly, or $29 month to month |

## Where they really differ

### The platform

Screen Studio is a native Mac app, and its site says there are no near-term plans for Windows. Lensprout runs wherever desktop Chrome runs. That is the biggest deciding factor for many people: if you are not on a Mac, Screen Studio isn't an option.

### What the zoom can see

A native app can follow your pointer across the whole desktop. A browser extension can only see pointer and keyboard activity inside web pages in Chrome. Lensprout can still record a whole screen or a window through Chrome's picker, but automatic zoom only follows activity on web pages; elsewhere you place zooms by hand in the editor. If your demos live in Figma's desktop app, Xcode or a terminal, Screen Studio's approach fits better.

### Polish versus ownership

Screen Studio has features Lensprout does not: cursor smoothing, webcam background removal, a podcast audio mode and hosted share links. Lensprout's strengths are elsewhere:

- **Price:** everything is free, including commercial use.
- **Openness:** the code is MIT licensed, so you can audit it or change it.
- **No cloud:** nothing is ever uploaded, because there is no server.
- **Editing extras:** smart trim suggestions, a 3D stage with device frames, generated click and key sounds, and presets.

### Vertical video

Both handle vertical output. Screen Studio offers 16:9, 1:1 and 9:16 plus a preview of social media overlays. Lensprout's "fill and follow" mode turns a wide recording into a 9:16 video and pans to keep the cursor in frame. See our [vertical video guide](/blog/vertical-9-16-video-from-screen-recording/).

## Availability

Screen Studio is available now for macOS. Lensprout is coming soon to the Chrome Web Store.

## Verdict

On a Mac, recording native apps, with budget for a subscription? Screen Studio is a strong, polished choice. Recording web apps on any desktop, and want something free you can inspect? Try Lensprout. For more options, read the [2026 roundup](/blog/best-auto-zoom-screen-recorders-2026/).
